# Competitor Analysis

## Overview

A structured competitive intelligence methodology covering the full lifecycle: competitor identification (direct, indirect, aspirational, emerging), digital audit (website UX, SEO performance, social media presence, advertising strategy), SWOT analysis per competitor, pricing and business model comparison, positioning map creation, content gap analysis, and ongoing monitoring. This skill provides the frameworks, templates, and analysis techniques to build a systematic competitive intelligence program that informs product, marketing, and strategic decisions.

## When to Use

- Conducting a competitive landscape review as part of strategic planning
- Preparing for a product launch or market entry
- Identifying content and SEO gaps vs. competitors
- Analyzing competitor pricing, positioning, and messaging
- Building a competitive monitoring and alerting system
- Informing sales enablement with competitive battle cards
- Evaluating acquisition targets or partnership opportunities
- Understanding market positioning and identifying whitespace opportunities
- Preparing investor or board presentations on competitive landscape

## Body

### 1. Competitor Identification & Classification

#### 1.1 Competitor Tiers

| Tier | Definition | Examples | Analysis Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Same product, same audience, same geography | Direct substitute — customer chooses between you and them | Full deep-dive (all sections) |
| **Indirect** | Different product, same audience and need | Solves same problem differently (e.g., CRM vs. spreadsheet) | Moderate (positioning, pricing, content) |
| **Aspirational** | More successful brand in your space | The "gold standard" you want to become | Light (best practices, strategy) |
| **Emergent** | New entrants or startups gaining traction | Threat in 12–24 months | Light (monitor, watchlist) |
| **Legacy** | Established but declining approaches | The old way of doing things | Light (transition analysis) |

**Identification framework:**

| Source | Method | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer surveys** | "Who else did you consider?" | Direct competitor list |
| **Sales team** | "Who do we lose to most often?" | Win/loss analysis, direct competitors |
| **Search (SERP)** | Search for your primary keywords | SEO competitors (may be indirect) |
| **Review sites** | G2, Capterra, Trustpilot — compare alternatives | Verified direct competitors |
| **Social listening** | "I wish [competitor] had..." | Indirect + aspirational |
| **Funding data** | Crunchbase, PitchBook — similar-stage companies | Emerging competitors |
| **App/shop directories** | App Store, Chrome Web Store, Shopify App Store | Niche/adjacent competitors |
| **Industry reports** | Gartner Magic Quadrant, Forrester Wave | All tiers |

### 5. Positioning Map

#### 5.1 2×2 Positioning Matrix

Plot all direct competitors on two axes:

**Common axis pairs:**
- Price (Low → High) × Features (Basic → Premium)
- Enterprise Fit (SMB → Enterprise) × Price (Low → High)
- Innovation (Traditional → Cutting Edge) × Price (Budget → Premium)
- Ease of Use (Hard → Easy) × Features (Basic → Rich)
- Niche (Generalist → Specialist) × Scale (Local → Global)

```
                    High Features / Premium
                           │
          [Comp C]         │           [Comp A]
                           │
                           │
        Niche ─────────────┼──────────── Broad
                           │
                           │
          [Us]             │           [Comp B]
                           │
                    Low Features / Budget
```

**Whitespace analysis:** Empty quadrants or under-populated areas represent potential positioning opportunities.

### 6. Content Gap Analysis

#### 6.1 Content Coverage Matrix

Map keywords and topics by coverage:

```
| Topic Cluster       | Us         | Comp A     | Comp B     | Opp. Level   |
|---------------------|------------|------------|------------|--------------|
| [Pillar topic 1]    | Pillar ✅  | Pillar ✅  | No pillar  | Medium (fix) |
| [Subtopic 1a]       | Cluster ✅ | Cluster ✅ | Cluster ✅ | Saturated    |
| [Subtopic 1b]       | ❌ Gap     | Cluster ✅ | ❌ Gap     | High — target|
| [Pillar topic 2]    | No pillar  | Pillar ✅  | Pillar ✅  | High — build |
| [Subtopic 2a]       | ❌ Gap     | ❌ Gap     | Cluster ✅ | Medium       |
| [Pillar topic 3]    | No pillar  | No pillar  | No pillar  | VERY HIGH    |
```

**Gap priority scoring:**

```
Priority = (Search Volume × 0.4) + (Intent Fit × 0.3) + (Comp Coverage × 0.2) + (Ease to Create × 0.1)

Where:
- Search Volume: 1–10 (based on keyword volume)
- Intent Fit: 1–10 (how well it matches our offering)
- Comp Coverage: 1–10 (higher = more competitors cover it = more urgent to compete or find angle)
- Ease: 1–10 (easier = higher score)
```

#### 6.2 SERP Feature Gap Analysis

For target keywords, check which SERP features competitors capture:

| Keyword | Featured Snippet | People Also Ask | Image Pack | Video | Local Pack | Top Ad |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "K" | Comp A | — | Comp B | Comp A | — | Comp C |
| "L" | — | Us, Comp A | — | Comp B | — | — |
| "M" | Comp A | Comp A, Comp B | — | — | — | Comp B |

**Strategy:** For each keyword where competitors have SERP features and we don't, create content optimized for that feature type (e.g., step-by-step format for "how to" snippets, FAQ schema for PAA, video for video carousel).

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Too many competitors:** Tracking 20+ competitors dilutes focus. Maintain 3–5 direct and 3–5 indirect at most. Refresh the list quarterly.
2. **Analysis without action:** A beautifully formatted competitive matrix that sits in a shared drive is useless. Each analysis should produce clear action items and owners.
3. **Confirmation bias:** Cherry-picking data that makes your brand look good while ignoring competitor strengths. The SWOT analysis must be honest.
4. **Comparing your insider view to their public face:** You know your weaknesses intimately; competitors' weaknesses must be inferred. Be honest about data quality differences.
5. **Price-only comparisons:** Competing solely on price is a race to the bottom. Focus analysis on value differentiation, not just price differences.
6. **Static, one-time analysis:** Competitors change month to month. Set up ongoing monitoring (alerts, automated checks) rather than annual deep-dives.
7. **No sales enablement:** Competitive insights that don't reach the sales team are wasted. Create battle cards, update them quarterly, and train the team.
8. **Copying instead of differentiating:** Finding a competitor's feature and immediately planning to build the same thing is a trap. Understand why customers choose THEM before deciding to compete.
9. **Ignoring indirect competitors:** The biggest threat is often a company in an adjacent category (e.g., Slack vs. email). Map indirect substitutes.
10. **No structured data collection:** Ad-hoc competitive intelligence (random screenshots, occasional articles) produces unreliable insights. Set up systematic collection with templates and tools.
