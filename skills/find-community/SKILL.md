# Find Community

## Core Principle

**Start with community, not with a product idea.** The best businesses are built by people who are already deeply embedded in a community and notice a problem worth solving. You don't "find" a community — you already belong to several.

## Framework: Identify Your Communities

1. **What communities are you already a part of?** Professional groups, hobbies, online forums, local orgs, identity groups, etc.
2. **Where do you spend your time online?** Reddit, Discord, Slack, Twitter/X, forums, Facebook groups, Substacks, YouTube communities.
3. **What problems do you hear people complain about repeatedly?** Persistent pain points within communities you understand deeply.
4. **Which communities excite you to serve for years?** This isn't a weekend project.

## Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| Authenticity | Are you a genuine member? Do you understand their language and culture? |
| Pain | Is the problem painful enough people would pay for a solution? |
| Reachability | Can you reach these people directly? |
| Size | Is the niche small enough to dominate but large enough to sustain? |

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Inventing a community from scratch | Join and contribute to an existing one |
| Choosing for market size over interest | Passion sustains you; market size doesn't |
| Skipping community participation | Contribute before you sell |
| Targeting too broad an audience | Niche down to a specific, reachable group |

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Identified 1-3 communities you belong to
- [ ] For each: specific persistent problem documented
- [ ] For each: community's gathering places identified
- [ ] You're genuinely contributing to the community
- [ ] Problem is painful enough people would pay
