# Content Marketing Workflow

## Overview

An end-to-end content marketing workflow covering every stage from audience research and persona development through topic cluster strategy, editorial calendar management, content brief generation, production, distribution, repurposing, and performance measurement. This skill provides templates, checklists, and decision frameworks for building a scalable content engine that drives organic traffic, lead generation, and brand authority.

## When to Use

- Building a content marketing program from scratch
- Planning quarterly or monthly editorial calendars
- Creating content briefs for writers and freelancers
- Designing topic clusters and pillar page strategies
- Auditing existing content for gaps and repurposing opportunities
- Setting up content distribution and promotion workflows
- Measuring content performance and optimizing based on data
- Scaling content production without sacrificing quality

### 2. Topic Cluster & Pillar Page Strategy

#### 2.1 Cluster Architecture

```
Pillar Page (broad topic, comprehensive guide)
├── Cluster 1 (subtopic) → links to pillar
├── Cluster 2 (subtopic) → links to pillar
├── Cluster 3 (subtopic) → links to pillar
├── Cluster 4 (subtopic) → links to pillar
└── Cluster 5 (subtopic) → links to pillar
```

**Cluster rules:**
- One pillar page per core topic (2,500–5,000 words)
- 5–15 cluster pages per pillar (1,000–2,500 words each)
- Every cluster page links back to the pillar with relevant anchor text
- Pillar page links out to every cluster page
- All pages in the cluster target related keywords with shared search intent

#### 2.2 Pillar Page Structure

```
H1: Complete Guide to [Core Topic]
├── Introduction (why this topic matters)
├── H2: What Is [Core Topic]? (definition, overview)
├── H2: Why [Core Topic] Matters (benefits, statistics)
├── H2: Types of [Core Topic] (categorization)
│   ├── H3: Type 1 (with link to cluster page)
│   ├── H3: Type 2 (with link to cluster page)
│   └── H3: Type 3 (with link to cluster page)
├── H2: How to Implement [Core Topic] (steps, process)
├── H2: Best Practices (do's and don'ts)
├── H2: Common Mistakes (pitfalls to avoid)
├── H2: Tools & Resources (curated list)
├── H2: FAQ (5–10 related questions with brief answers)
└── H2: Conclusion (CTA to next step)
```

### 6. Distribution & Promotion Checklist

For every piece of content, execute the following distribution:

#### 6.1 Owned Channels

- [ ] Publish on blog / website with proper SEO (title, meta, alt text, schema)
- [ ] Feature in email newsletter (primary or secondary slot)
- [ ] Add to relevant "resource" or "learn" section if appropriate
- [ ] Cross-link from 2–3 existing related articles
- [ ] Add to content hub / pillar page if part of a cluster

#### 6.2 Earned Channels

- [ ] Share on LinkedIn (company page + personal profiles of team members)
- [ ] Share on Twitter/X (2–3 tweets over 48 hours)
- [ ] Share on relevant Facebook Groups / LinkedIn Groups
- [ ] Submit to industry newsletters (if original/research-based)
- [ ] Pitch to relevant aggregator sites (GrowthHackers, Inbound.org, HackerNews if applicable)
- [ ] Reach out to experts quoted in the piece (tag them, ask for share)

#### 6.3 Paid Channels

- [ ] Boost top-performing posts on LinkedIn (after 1 week of organic data)
- [ ] Retarget blog visitors with relevant ad content
- [ ] Promote via Google Discovery Ads if suitable
- [ ] Sponsored content in industry newsletters (for major pieces)

### 7. Performance Measurement

#### 7.1 Content KPIs by Funnel Stage

| Stage | KPI | Target | Measurement Tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Organic traffic to content | +20% MoM | Google Analytics |
| **Awareness** | Keyword rankings | Page 1 for 50% of target terms | Ahrefs / GSC |
| **Awareness** | Impressions | Non-branded growth | Google Search Console |
| **Consideration** | Time on page | > 3 minutes | Google Analytics |
| **Consideration** | Scroll depth | > 50% average | Hotjar / GA4 |
| **Consideration** | Social shares | > 50 per piece | Native platform |
| **Consideration** | Backlinks per piece | > 3 per piece (or 10+ for pillar) | Ahrefs / SEMrush |
| **Decision** | Content-to-lead conversion rate | > 3% | GA4 + CRM |
| **Decision** | MQLs from content | 20% of total MQLs | CRM source tracking |
| **Retention** | Newsletter signups from content | > 2% of visitors | ESP |
| **Retention** | Return visitor rate | > 25% | Google Analytics |

#### 7.2 Content Audit Framework

Quarterly, audit your content library:

1. **Crawl all published URLs** (Screaming Frog, Sitebulb)
2. **Score each piece** on: Traffic (0–10), Engagement (0–10), Conversions (0–10), SEO performance (0–10)
3. **Categorize** into:

| Score | Action | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| 30–40 (Star) | Keep, promote, repurpose, update every 6 months | High |
| 20–29 (Grow) | Optimize, update with new data, add internal links | Medium |
| 10–19 (Maintain) | Consolidate or redirect to better content | Quarterly review |
| 0–9 (Prune) | 301 redirect to relevant page or noindex | Quarterly |

4. **Identify gaps:** Topics you haven't covered that competitors have
5. **Plan refresh:** Update top 20% of underperforming content annually

## Common Pitfalls

1. **No content briefs:** Writing without a brief produces inconsistent quality and missed SEO targets. Always provide a structured brief.
2. **Inconsistent publishing cadence:** Posting 3 articles one week and nothing for 3 weeks kills audience trust. Set a sustainable cadence and stick to it.
3. **Creating without distributing:** The #1 mistake. Spend 20% of time creating, 80% distributing. A great piece with no distribution is invisible.
4. **Ignoring content repurposing:** One blog post can become 10+ pieces of content. Not repurposing is leaving 80% of your content ROI on the table.
5. **Writing for everyone (writing for no one):** Generic content targeting a broad audience fails. Write for a specific persona with specific pains.
6. **No SEO optimization upfront:** Fixing SEO after publishing is harder than building it in from the brief stage. Brief should include keyword, title, meta, headings.
7. **Vanity metrics over business impact:** Page views don't pay the bills. Track content-to-lead conversion, content-influenced revenue, and MQL attribution.
8. **No formal review process:** Publishing unchecked content damages credibility. At minimum: writer → editor → publish. For data-heavy pieces, add fact-check.
9. **Topic sprawl:** Covering too many unrelated topics prevents building topical authority. Go deep on fewer topics rather than shallow on many.
10. **Not updating older content:** Your best traffic often comes from pages published 1–3 years ago. Neglecting updates lets them decay in rankings.

- [ ] Content library has been audited in the last 3 months
- [ ] Repurposing pipeline active (blog → social → email → PDF)
- [ ] Publishing cadence established and sustainable
